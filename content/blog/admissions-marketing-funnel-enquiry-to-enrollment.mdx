Admissions is a funnel with a deadline. This guide covers how I structure it, from the first campaign to the enrolled student. See also [admissions marketing](/services/admissions-marketing/).

## The funnel

### 1. Plan the intake
List programmes, seat targets and key dates. Decide which segments of students you are trying to reach for each.

### 2. Run online and offline outreach together
Digital campaigns, school and community outreach, events and referrals all produce enquiries. Give each channel a way to be tracked, even if it is a simple source code on a form.

### 3. Capture every enquiry in one place
One sheet or CRM, with a source on every row. Offline enquiries must be entered the same day or they disappear.

### 4. Segment and score
Group enquiries by programme and profile, then score how ready each one is. See how a [segmented and scored lead pipeline](/blog/building-a-segmented-scored-lead-pipeline/) works.

### 5. Counsellor follow-up
Route by score band with clear timelines, so ready students are called first and others enter a reminder sequence.

### 6. Work with the admissions team
Agree stage definitions (enquiry, application, counselling, enrolled) so marketing and admissions are talking about the same thing.

### 7. Report by programme and channel
Review before the next intake: which channels produced enrolments, not just enquiries.

## FAQ

### When should admissions campaigns start?

Well before application deadlines so there is time to test and improve. Exact timing depends on your calendar.

### How do I track offline enquiries?

Give each offline channel a source code and record enquiries in the same sheet or CRM as online ones.

### What is the most important funnel metric?

Enrolments by channel and programme, not total enquiries.

### Do I need a CRM?

A well-structured sheet works to begin with. A CRM helps as volume grows.
