<KeyTakeaways>

- Write a hypothesis, change one variable and decide your success rule before launch.
- Judge tests on cost per lead and lead quality, not clicks.
- Keep a test log so each test builds on the last.

</KeyTakeaways>

Most Meta accounts run lots of ads but few real tests. The method below is the structure I use to make each test teach something. If you want it run for your business, see [Meta ads and performance marketing](/services/meta-ads-performance-marketing/).

## The method

### 1. Baseline
Record current cost per lead, lead volume and what happens to those leads. You cannot claim an improvement without a starting point.

### 2. Hypothesis
Write it as: if we change **one variable**, then **this metric** will improve because **reason**.

### 3. One variable
Choose one: offer, creative, headline, audience or lead form. Keep everything else identical and split the audience so variants do not overlap.

### 4. Decide the rule in advance
Before launch, agree the budget, the duration and the statistical confidence level you will require. Then do not stop early because one variant looks ahead on day two. A result only counts if it meets the rule you set.

### 5. Judge on cost and quality
A cheaper lead nobody can reach is not a win. Ask sales how the leads from each variant behaved.

### 6. Log and repeat

## FAQ

### What is statistical significance in ad testing?

It is a way of checking that a difference between two variants is unlikely to be luck. Decide the confidence level you require before the test starts.

### What should I test first?

Usually the offer and the opening line, then creative format, audience and the lead form.

### Is the lowest cost per lead always best?

No. Check lead quality with your sales team.

### Do I need a big budget to A/B test?

No, but run fewer tests with bigger differences between variants, and give each enough time.
