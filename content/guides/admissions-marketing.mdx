<KeyTakeaways>

- Plan backwards from deadlines and seat targets by programme.
- Run online and offline outreach together, and track both in one place.
- Segment and score enquiries so counsellors call the most ready students first.
- Measure enrolments by channel, not just enquiries.

</KeyTakeaways>

Admissions is a funnel with a deadline: enquiries come in, a share become applications, a share of those enrol. This guide is how I structure that funnel for institutions in India. It draws on my admissions marketing work; for help, see [admissions marketing](/services/admissions-marketing/).

## 1. Plan backwards from the calendar

List each programme, its seat target, application deadlines and counselling dates. Work backwards: when must campaigns start to leave time for testing? Which programmes need the most help?

## 2. Know your audience segments

Students and parents are two audiences with different questions. Typical segments: by programme interest, by location, by the stage they are at (exploring, shortlisting, deciding). Write one sentence for each: what they worry about and what would convince them.

## 3. Run online and offline together

| Channel | Examples | How to track |
|---|---|---|
| Paid social and search | Lead forms, landing pages | Source and campaign on every lead |
| Organic and content | Website, social posts, WhatsApp | Source codes on forms and links |
| Offline | School visits, fairs, referrals, walk-ins | Source code recorded at the desk |
| Partnerships | Coaching centres, alumni | A named referrer on each record |

Offline enquiries are the easiest to lose. Enter them the same day.

## 4. Capture every enquiry in one place

One sheet or CRM, one record per student, with source, programme, contact details and status. Agree stage names with the admissions team, for example: **enquiry → counselled → applied → enrolled**, so marketing and admissions mean the same thing.

## 5. Segment and score

Score on fit (programme match, eligibility, location) and intent (asked for a call, visited the campus page, replied). Bands decide who counsellors call first. See how this works in [lead generation with AI](/guides/lead-generation-with-ai/).

## 6. Counsellor follow-up

- Set a target time for the first call by band.
- Give counsellors a call script and a short list of objections with honest answers.
- Use reminders for the middle of the funnel, where students go quiet.
- Log the outcome of every call.

## 7. Report by programme and channel

Weekly: enquiries, calls made, applications. Monthly: enrolments by channel and programme, and the cost per application. Share it with the admissions team, and use it to decide where to spend before the next intake.

## 8. Template: weekly admissions review

| Question | Where to look |
|---|---|
| Which programmes are behind their targets? | Enrolments versus seat target |
| Which channels produce applications, not just enquiries? | Source by stage |
| Where are students dropping out? | Stage-to-stage conversion |
| What is the response time? | Time to first call |
| What will we change this week? | One action per programme |

## 9. Common mistakes

- Judging channels by enquiries instead of enrolments
- Slow first response to hot enquiries
- Offline enquiries not recorded
- Marketing and admissions using different stage definitions
- Starting campaigns too close to deadlines

## FAQ

### When should admissions campaigns start?

Well ahead of application deadlines, so there is time to test and improve. The exact timing depends on your calendar.

### How do I track offline enquiries?

Give each offline channel a source code and enter enquiries in the same sheet or CRM as online ones.

### What is the most important admissions metric?

Enrolments by channel and programme.

### Do schools and colleges need different approaches?

The structure is the same. The audience, language and decision-makers differ.

### How do counsellors decide who to call first?

By score band: hot enquiries first, with a target response time, then warm, then cold.
