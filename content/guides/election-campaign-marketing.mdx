<KeyTakeaways>

- Start from the electorate and their concerns, not from slogans.

- Coordinate online and offline work and assign an owner to each channel.
- Follow the election authority's rules and every platform's political-advertising policy.

This guide is a neutral planning framework for the marketing and communications side of an election. It does not endorse any party, candidate or panel, and it applies equally to association, institutional and public elections. For related work, see [election campaign marketing](/services/election-campaign-marketing/).

## 1. Understand the electorate

- **Who can vote?** Eligibility and the size of the electorate.
- **How do they get information?** Direct contact, groups, social platforms, printed material.
- **What do they care about?** Talk to a sample before you write a word of messaging.

In small electorates, listening is the most valuable and most neglected step.

## 2. Write a clear manifesto

A good manifesto is short and specific. For each commitment, answer: *what exactly, by when, and how would a voter check?* A few achievable commitments beat a long list of promises. Have people outside the team read it and tell you what they understood.

## 3. Build the message from the manifesto

Keep one consistent message across every channel: posters, meetings, messages and posts. Test two or three wordings with a small group before wide release, and keep language respectful and factual.

## 4. Choose channels and owners

| Channel | Use | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Direct conversations and meetings | Trust, answering questions | Named volunteers |
| Printed material | Visibility, reference | One coordinator |
| Messaging groups | Fast updates | Admins with clear rules |
| Social platforms | Reach, explanation | A single content owner |
| Paid digital | Targeted reach, where permitted | Marketing lead |

Assign each channel an owner and a weekly plan.

## 5. Capture support, with consent

Keep a simple list of supporters and volunteers. Say clearly how details will be used, collect only what you need, and respect requests to be removed. Store the list securely and limit who can see it.

## 6. Follow the rules

Check, before launch and again during the campaign:

- The election authority's rules on campaigning and spending
- Each platform's policies on political and social-issue advertising
- Rules on printed material, permissions and disclosures
- Data protection obligations for supporter lists

When in doubt, ask for guidance in writing. Do not publish claims about opponents that you cannot support.

## 7. Plan the weeks

Work back from polling day: early weeks for listening and the manifesto, middle weeks for reach and conversations, final days for reminders and logistics. Leave slack for surprises.

## 8. Daily review template

| Question | Note |
|---|---|
| What did we do today, by channel? | |
| What responses and questions came in? | |
| What did we learn about concerns? | |
| What is the plan for tomorrow? | |
| Any rule or policy issues to check? | |

A short daily note beats a long weekly report, because decisions are needed daily.

## 9. Measuring a campaign

The outcome arrives only on election day, so track what you control: conversations held, supporters captured, questions answered, material distributed and follow-ups completed. Treat these as indicators of effort and reach, not predictions.

## 10. Common mistakes

- Slogans before listening
- A long, vague manifesto
- No owner for a channel
- Collecting supporter data without consent or security
- Ignoring platform advertising policies

## FAQ

### Is election campaign marketing different from normal marketing?

The tools are similar, but the rules, the stakes and the need for neutrality and consent are different. Compliance comes first.

### How long should a manifesto be?

As short as possible while staying specific. A voter should be able to read it in a few minutes.

### Can digital ads be used in election campaigns?

Where the law and the platform allow it, and under each platform's political-advertising rules.

### How do you measure a campaign?

By activity and reach indicators you control, reviewed daily, since the result only arrives on election day.

### How should supporter data be handled?

With clear consent, only the details needed, secure storage and a way to opt out.
