<KeyTakeaways>

- Lead generation fails more often at follow-up than at capture. Fix the pipeline, not just the ads.
- Clean and deduplicate first, then segment (who), then score (how ready).
- AI is most useful for classifying free text, enriching records and drafting follow-ups, not for replacing your sales judgement.
- Measure cost per qualified lead, not cost per click.

</KeyTakeaways>

Most businesses I speak to do not have a shortage of enquiries; they have a prioritisation problem. This guide lays out a pipeline for turning enquiries into customers, and shows where AI helps. For hands-on help, see [AI lead generation](/services/ai-lead-generation/).

## The pipeline at a glance

**Capture → Clean → Deduplicate → Segment → Score → Route → Follow up → Review**

Each step has an owner and a measure. Skipping any of them is where leads go cold.

## 1. Capture

Collect enquiries from every channel into one place: forms, ad lead forms, calls, WhatsApp, events and referrals. Put the source on every record. Source is one of the strongest signals you will have.

**Template: minimum fields**

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Name, phone, email | Contact, and the key for deduplication |
| Source and campaign | Which channel produced it |
| Date and time | Speed to first response |
| Interest or product | Segment and message |
| Location | Territory and language |
| Free-text message | AI can classify this |

## 2. Clean and deduplicate

Standardise phone numbers and emails, fix capitalisation and map free-text values to a short list. Then deduplicate: match on phone or email first, then on name plus company. Decide which record wins and keep a log of merges so mistakes can be undone. Duplicates make scores unreliable and cause the embarrassing double call.

## 3. Segment

Group by what the lead is: sector, size, location, product interest. Three to five segments is enough. Segments decide the message and the owner.

## 4. Score: fit plus intent

Keep two scores so you can see why a lead ranks high.

- **Fit:** does this lead resemble the customers you serve best?
- **Intent:** have they asked for a call, visited the pricing page, replied to a message?

**Template: a starter scoring sheet** (the points are illustrations; set yours from your own data)

| Signal | Type | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Matches a target segment | Fit | +20 |
| Budget in range | Fit | +15 |
| Asked for a call | Intent | +30 |
| Opened two or more emails | Intent | +10 |
| Incomplete or invalid contact details | Fit | -10 |

Ask sales which leads they wish they had called first and see which signals those leads share. Then turn points into three bands: hot, warm and cold.

## 5. Route and follow up

- **Hot:** a named person, a same-day call.
- **Warm:** an automated sequence plus a personal message.
- **Cold:** a slower nurture, reviewed monthly.

Write the response-time targets down and measure them.

## 6. Where AI actually helps

- **Classifying free-text enquiries** into segments or product interest, reviewed on a sample each month.
- **Enrichment:** filling gaps from public information, within the law and your privacy policy.
- **Drafting follow-ups** that a person edits and approves.
- **Summarising conversations** for the next person who picks up the lead.

AI does not fix a bad offer, slow follow-up or missing data. See the broader picture in the [AI marketing automation guide](/guides/ai-marketing-automation/).

## 7. Review: close the loop

Once a month, compare score bands with outcomes. If hot leads do not convert better than warm ones, the model is wrong. Adjust the rules and note what changed.

## What to measure

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| Cost per lead | Efficiency of acquisition |
| Share of leads that are qualified | Quality |
| Time to first response | The biggest controllable lever |
| Conversion by band and source | Whether the score works |
| Cost per customer | The number that matters |

You can work these out with the [lead cost calculator](/resources/lead-cost-calculator/).

## FAQ

### What is lead generation with AI?

Using automation and language models to capture, clean, classify, score and follow up on enquiries so the right ones reach sales quickly.

### Do I need a CRM?

A well-structured sheet is enough to start. A CRM helps once volume grows and routing needs to be automated.

### What is the difference between lead scoring and segmentation?

Segmentation groups leads by what they are. Scoring ranks them by how ready they are to buy.

### How many scoring rules should I use?

Five to eight, written in plain language so sales can challenge them.

### How do I know the scoring works?

Compare score bands with real conversions each month, and adjust the rules where they disagree.
